A working demonstration for a state arts agency

A second reader that says how sure it is.

An arts agency reads for a living: applications against guidelines, narratives against rubrics, hundreds of final reports against the same handful of questions. A calibrated judgment model can take a first pass at that reading. It does not write and it does not decide. It answers a typed question and attaches a probability, so the uncertain cases go to a person.

How to use this site

  1. 1
    Pick a roomEach one is a job the agency already does. A room takes about two minutes. There is nothing to sign in to.
  2. 2
    Use the sample or write your ownEvery room opens with a fictional document already loaded. You can edit it or replace it with invented text.
  3. 3
    Press the button, then read the doubtJev answers in a few seconds. Look at the percentages, not only the answer. The unsure ones are the point.

Where it stays out

Artistic merit

Fellowships and work samples belong to peer panels. A probability on a poem is false precision, and nothing in these rooms scores art.

Final decisions

The model never declines, funds, or ranks. Every room ends with a person, and the uncertain cases are the ones it hands over first.

Hidden use

A public agency owes applicants disclosure, a named decision-maker, and a way to appeal. The rooms show what was asked and what came back.

Polish over substance

These models reward writing that sounds like the rubric. That can favor a hired grant writer over a small rural group, so splits get read by people.

Disclosure

What this is, who made it, and where your words go.

Who made this

Second Reader was conceived and directed by Marc Watkins. It is an independent demonstration. It is not affiliated with, commissioned by, or endorsed by the South Carolina Arts Commission, and it is not a system the Commission uses.

AI disclosure

The site's design, code, and explanatory text were written with Claude, an AI system made by Anthropic, working from Marc Watkins's direction. Treat every claim here as a starting point to check, not as policy advice.

The judgments you see in the rooms come from Jev, a model made by TypeSafe AI, and are labeled as Jev's. Jev does not generate prose. It answers yes or no, multiple choice, and scale questions, returns a probability with each, and for images can return a box. Sage, from Levanto, is a similar kind of tool. Only Jev is connected here.

What is fictional

Every sample application, draft, and final report was written by AI for this demonstration. The organizations and people named in them do not exist. The counties are real. Nothing here describes a real applicant or grantee.

What is real

Grant names, eligibility rules, funding limits, and rubric criterion names and point ranges follow the Commission's published FY27 guidelines. The band descriptions under each criterion are paraphrased for this demo and are not the official wording. Read the official text at the source:

    On November 3, 2026, the Commission paused applications for its rolling grant categories, including Art of Community: Rural SC. That category is used here only as a published example of rules and a rubric.

    Where your words go

    1. Your browser. Text you type and scores you give are saved only in this browser so a refresh does not lose them. There are no accounts and no analytics.
    2. A small relay. When you press a button that asks Jev, the document and the questions go to a Cloudflare Worker run by Marc Watkins. It holds the API key, stores nothing, and does not log the text.
    3. TypeSafe AI. The relay forwards the request to TypeSafe's API and returns the answer. TypeSafe's own terms govern what it keeps. This site cannot verify them for you.

    Do not paste real applications, personal information, or anything confidential. Use the samples or invented text. If an agency adopted a tool like this, it would need its own agreement with the vendor, a records policy, and legal review.

    Fonts load from Google Fonts and sample photographs load from Wikimedia Commons, so those services see a request from your browser.

    Known limits

    • A probability is the model's confidence, not the chance it is right about your case. It can be confidently wrong.
    • Documents are limited to about 6,000 characters and eight questions per request. The relay allows 60 requests a minute.
    • Image and box features are marked experimental by the vendor and may change.
    • No one has tested these questions for bias against real applications. That test would come before any real use.
